What happened between rounds: almost nothing

The same concerns, raised again

Matched by meaning (prebuttal diff --llm), all 8 items of the v1 revision plan were raised again against v2: headline claims beyond the tested scale (scaling laws stop near 1.3B, zero-shot at 2.8B), no seeds or error bars in any table, a non-monotonic Table 13, a missing Transformer++ row in Table 3, efficiency numbers without operating context. The one new v2 item is the portability of the custom fused CUDA kernel. To this panel, the revision did not address what it cared about.

The score doesn't move either — and that is measured, not guessed

Each version was reviewed three times (seeds 42–44). v1: 7.33, 7.33, 7.00 (mean 7.22). v2: 7.33, 7.33, 7.33 (mean 7.33). The whole difference is one point from one reviewer in one run — inside the noise. The senior and detail reviewers gave 8 to both versions in every run: this panel does not separate the two versions, and an earlier version of this page that read a 7.0 → 7.3 “improvement” from single runs was reading noise.

A claim we withdrew

That earlier page also said a “Mamba-3B vs 2.8B” naming inconsistency was “gone in v2, because the revision fixed it”. It is not: the seed-42 v2 review raises it again. The old claim came from word-overlap matching, which on this pair calls 5 of the 8 plan items resolved — by meaning, none were. That is why diff --llm exists.

The area chair still checks its panel

In both rounds the AC (claude opus) sided with the two 8s but kept the adversarial reviewer's points on scoping the claims. In v2 it disputed part of R2's case: the decisive Table 3 comparisons share tokenizer, data and token budget, and R2's reading of Table 15 conflated a memory benchmark with the KV-cache argument.

Read this honestly: frontier models have almost certainly seen Mamba in training (contamination), which pushes scores toward the lenient side. And arXiv v2 being accepted at a different venue says little about whether it answered these particular concerns. What this demo does show is the workflow — run, revise, diff, re-check with more seeds — and a tool that reports “no change” when it sees none.
