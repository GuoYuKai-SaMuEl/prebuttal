What happened between rounds: almost nothing

The score moves less than the noise — and the noise is measured

Each version is one prebuttal run with repeats = 3: every reviewer reviews three times and the area chair once. The round means are 7.67, 7.67, 7.67 for v1 and 8, 7.67, 8 for v2. prebuttal diff reports 7.67 → 7.89 (Δ +0.22) as within run-to-run noise (±0.3, measured: 3 and 3 rounds). The movement comes from two reviewers: the detail reviewer went from 7 to 8, the senior reviewer gave one 7 among its 8s. This panel does not separate the two versions.

The same concerns, raised again

Matched by meaning, all 8 items of the v1 revision plan were raised again against v2: two mistaken Average cells in Table 3 (Mamba-370M and 790M), headline claims beyond the tested scale (Transformer++ parity only up to about 1.3B), no variance estimates in any table while conclusions rest on small gaps, narrow long-context evidence (DNA against HyenaDNA only, a non-monotonic Table 13), efficiency numbers that depend on large batches without a KV cache, missing baselines, unablated block components. The one new v2 item: Section 1 carries a non-anonymised GitHub link, a double-blind concern on a public arXiv version. Of the individual weaknesses, 40 persist, 5 are new and 6 are gone, nearly all of the new and gone ones raised in one round of three — chance, as a single review is.

No dissenter this time

The real ICLR 2024 panel had a 3. The adversarial reviewer here gave 8 in all six of its rounds; on the earlier panel of this page (gpt-6-luna) it gave 6. A persona sets the tone, not the verdict: a model that finds the paper strong says so even when told to probe. That is one more reason not to read these scores as a prediction of the real decision.

The area chair still checks its panel

In both rounds the AC (claude opus 5.5) recommended acceptance but kept the detail reviewer's points as required fixes: it recomputed the Table 3 averages and sided with R3, the only reviewer who found them. It also rejected an objection of R1 and R2 to the Table 3 caption ("best-in-class on every single evaluation"): their counter-examples compare across size classes, and within each size the caption holds.

A claim we withdrew

An earlier version of this page said a “Mamba-3B vs 2.8B” naming inconsistency was “gone in v2, because the revision fixed it”. It was not. The claim came from word-overlap matching, which on that pair called 5 of the 8 plan items resolved — by meaning, none were. That is why diff matches by meaning by default since 0.7. The same version read a 7.0 → 7.3 “improvement” from single runs; three seeds run by hand then showed it was noise, which repeats now measures in one run.

Read this honestly: frontier models have almost certainly seen Mamba in training (contamination), which pushes scores toward the lenient side. And arXiv v2 being accepted at a different venue says little about whether it answered these particular concerns. What this demo does show is the workflow — run, revise, diff, with the noise measured — and a tool that reports “no change” when it sees none.
