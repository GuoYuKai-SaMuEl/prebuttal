What moved between rounds

The strictest reviewer is the one that moved

Only the adversarial reviewer changed score (5 → 6) and the panel's disagreement narrowed (σ 1.41 → 0.94) — the same direction as the real outcome, where the rejected paper was later accepted in revised form. The sensitivity is real but modest: a stress test, not a prophecy.

Weaknesses the revision actually fixed disappeared

The v1 panel flagged a “Mamba-3B vs Mamba-2.8B” naming inconsistency — gone in v2, because the revision fixed it. The loudest persistent weakness, “no variance or error bars anywhere”, survived both rounds — exactly what the real ICLR reviewers hammered on too. In v2 the new critiques refocus on long-context retrieval evidence, matching the restructured paper (33 → 19 sections).

The area chair fact-checks its panel

In v1 the AC (claude opus) refused to count half-empty tables against the authors, correctly judging them an extraction artifact of the tool rather than the paper — and in v2 it caught the adversarial reviewer mis-reading the OPT-6.7B comparison in Table 3, quoting the correct numbers from the paper.
