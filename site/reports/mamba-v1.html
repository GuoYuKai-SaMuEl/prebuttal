## Summary of the submission The paper argues that linear time-invariant (LTI) structured SSMs (S4, H3, Hyena) cannot perform content-based selection, which hurts them on discrete, information-dense data. It makes the SSM parameters input-dependent: Delta, B and C become functions of x (Algorithm 2, 'S6'). This breaks the convolutional form, so the authors add a hardware-aware selective scan that combines kernel fusion, parallel scan and recomputation. The scan keeps the expanded (B, L, D, N) state in SRAM rather than HBM (Section 3.3). The layer sits inside a homogeneous block that merges the H3 block with a gated MLP (Section 3.4, Figure 3). The evaluation covers: - synthetic tasks (Tables 1, 2, 11); - Pile scaling from about 125M to 1.3B parameters (Figure 4, Table 12); - zero-shot downstream tasks up to 2.8B (Table 3); - DNA (Figure 5, Table 13); - audio (Figure 7, Tables 4 and 5); - efficiency (Figure 8, Table 15); - ablations at about 350M (Tables 6-10). ## Assessment of the evidence **Core idea and mechanism (strong, all reviewers agree).** - Section 3.1 frames selection as compression, and Section 3.5.1 (Theorem 1) ties it to RNN gating; both give a crisp motivation. - The ablations isolate the mechanism. Table 6 shows S6 improving clearly over every LTI layer, while LTI variants are nearly indistinguishable. Table 7 identifies Delta as the most important selective parameter. - Increasing state size N under selection gives a large gain: perplexity drops from 9.73 to 8.71 while parameters grow from 367.1M to 371.5M, about 1.2%. This matches the stated motivation of 'over 1.0 perplexity for 1% parameters'. - On Induction Heads (Table 11), a 74K-parameter Mamba generalizes perfectly from training length 2^8 to 2^20.
