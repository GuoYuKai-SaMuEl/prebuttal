AC estimate: 8/10

Summary

The submission proposes selective SSMs (S6), in which Δ, B and C are functions of the input (Algorithm 2). This breaks the LTI/convolutional form, so the paper adds a hardware-aware fused scan that keeps the expanded (B,L,D,N) state in SRAM (Section 3.3.2). It also introduces a homogeneous Mamba block (Section 3.4). The evaluation covers synthetic tasks, Pile language modeling (scaling laws to ~1.3B in Figure 4; zero-shot to 2.8B in Table 3), DNA, audio, efficiency and ablations.

All three reviewers recommend acceptance (R1: 8, R2: 8, R3: 7, each consistent across all three of its rounds). None of them identified a flaw that invalidates the central claims. I agree with that assessment.

What is well supported

The mechanism is isolated cleanly.

  • Table 1: S4→S6 raises selective-copying accuracy from 18.3→97.0 with no gate, and 56.4→99.8 in the Mamba block. Gating alone is insufficient (H3+S4 57.0).
  • Table 11: Mamba is the only model with perfect induction-head extrapolation from 2^8 to 2^20.
  • Table 6: the LTI layers cluster at 10.24–10.75 perplexity, while S6 reaches 8.95 (H3 block) and 8.69 (Mamba block).
  • Table 10 is the most convincing piece of evidence. Increasing N from 1 to 16 helps only when B and C are selective (9.73→8.71) and barely helps when they are constant (9.88→9.81). This directly supports the motivation in Sections 3.1 and 3.3.

The language results are strong against matched baselines.

  • Table 3: Mamba-2.8B averages 63.3, versus 59.1 for Pythia-2.8B, 59.6 for RWKV-3B and 61.7 for Pythia-6.9B. Pythia and RWKV share the tokenizer, data and 300B-token budget (Section 4.2.2).
