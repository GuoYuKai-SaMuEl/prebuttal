## Summary The submission proposes GlobalGate, which multiplies each attention head's output by a learned scalar g_h (Section 2). It claims this "solves long-context reasoning" and is "universal" across "any architecture, task and modality" (Abstract, Section 4). The only empirical evidence is Table 1: on LongQA-Mini, a new set of 200 questions written by the authors, GlobalGate trained for 5 epochs reaches 74.0%. The vanilla Transformer baseline, trained by the authors for 1 epoch, reaches 61.0%. Both models have 125M parameters. Each was trained once (seed 0) at a 2k-token context. All three reviewers recommend rejection as technically flawed (R1: 1, R2: 2, R3: 1, all at confidence 5). Their critiques largely overlap, and every critical point can be checked against sentences the authors wrote in Section 3.1 or 3.2. So the unanimity reflects verifiable defects rather than shared harshness. ## Decisive, verifiable flaws 1. **Confounded comparison (R1, R2, R3).** Section 3.1 states "The baseline is a vanilla Transformer trained by us for 1 epoch; GlobalGate is trained for 5 epochs." - The 13-point gap in Table 1 (74.0 vs 61.0) cannot be attributed to the gate when the method received 5x the training. - This alone invalidates the only result in the paper. 2. **Evaluation on the tuning set (R1, R2, R3).** Section 3.1: "we use the same 200 questions to select hyperparameters and to report results." - The 74.0% is therefore not a held-out estimate. - The section does not say whether the baseline received comparable tuning. 3. **No statistical support (R1, R2, R3).** There is one seed and n=200, with no intervals. - R1 notes the standard error at n=200 is roughly 3 points.
