## Summary of the submission GlobalGate multiplies each attention head's output by a learned, input-independent scalar passed through a sigmoid (Eq. 1). The paper claims this "solves long-context reasoning", outperforms "all existing methods on every benchmark", and is "universal" across "any architecture, task and modality" (Abstract, Section 4). The sole evidence is Table 1: - a vanilla Transformer scores 61.0% and GlobalGate scores 74.0%; - both models have 125M parameters; - the dataset is LongQA-Mini, 200 author-written questions at 2k-token context; - each model is trained once (seed 0). ## Assessment All three reviewers recommend rejection (R1: 1, R2: 2, R3: 1, all at confidence 5). Every central criticism can be checked directly against sentences the authors themselves wrote in Section 3.1 and Section 3.2. This is not a case of a harsh reviewer outvoting kinder ones. The flaws are self-reported in the manuscript, and they invalidate the paper's only result. **1. Confounded comparison (fatal).** Section 3.1 states that the baseline is trained for 1 epoch and GlobalGate for 5 epochs. The 13-point gap in Table 1 (61.0 vs 74.0) therefore cannot be attributed to Eq. (1). A 5× training budget alone is a sufficient explanation. All three reviewers flag this. No claim about the gate's effect survives without a budget-matched baseline. **2. Evaluation on the tuning set (fatal).** Section 3.1 says "we use the same 200 questions to select hyperparameters and to report results." The 74.0% is thus not a held-out estimate. All three reviewers raise this. **3.
