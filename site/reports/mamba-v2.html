## Summary of the submission The paper argues that linear-time sequence models (LTI SSMs, global convolutions, linear attention) underperform attention on discrete data because they cannot do content-based selection. It proposes making the SSM parameters Δ, B, C functions of the input (S6, Section 3.2). Because this breaks the convolutional computation path, it adds a hardware-aware scan that keeps the (B, L, D, N) expanded state in SRAM (Section 3.3). The layer sits inside a homogeneous block that merges the H3 block with a gated MLP (Mamba, Section 3.4). Experiments cover: - synthetic tasks (Tables 1, 11) - Pile language modeling, with scaling laws to ~1.3B (Fig. 4) and zero-shot evaluation to 2.8B (Table 3) - HG38 DNA (Fig. 5, Table 13) - audio (Fig. 7, Tables 4-5) - efficiency (Fig. 8, Table 15) - ablations (Tables 6-10) ## Assessment of the evidence All three reviewers agree on the core contribution, and I concur. The synthetic results isolate the mechanism cleanly: - **Selective Copying (Table 1).** S4 reaches 18.3% and S6 with no gate reaches 97.0%. Gating alone does not close the gap: H3+S4 gets 57.0 and Mamba-block+S4 gets 56.4. This directly supports the claim that architectural gating is not selection. - **Induction Heads (Table 11).** A 74K-parameter Mamba trained at length 256 is perfect up to 2^20. MHA variants fall to 5-10% by 2^12-2^14 and run out of memory from 2^15. - **Ablations.** They are well targeted: - Table 6: S6 gives 8.95/8.69 versus 10.24-10.75 for the LTI layers. - Table 7: full selectivity gives 8.71 versus 10.93 with none. - Table 10 is the most convincing mechanistic evidence.
