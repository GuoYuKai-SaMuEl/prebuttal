## Summary The submission proposes Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). The paper uses the closed-form optimum of the KL-constrained reward objective (Eq. 4) to write the reward as β log π/π_ref + β log Z(x) (Eq. 5). The partition function cancels in the Bradley-Terry likelihood (Eq. 6), so the policy can be trained with a binary cross-entropy loss (Eq. 7), with no explicit reward model and no RL sampling loop. Section 5.1 (Lemmas 1-2, Theorem 1, Proposition 1 in App. A.5-A.6) argues the reparameterization loses no generality over reward equivalence classes, given π_ref>0 and β>0. Experiments cover: - IMDb sentiment with a ground-truth classifier reward (reward-KL frontier, 22 runs, Fig. 2 left); - Reddit TL;DR with GPT-J (GPT-4 win rates, Fig. 2 right); - single-turn Anthropic-HH with Pythia-2.8B (Fig. 3); - a CNN/DailyMail transfer test (Table 1); - a human study validating GPT-4 as judge (Table 2). ## Assessment of the contribution All three reviewers agree the core idea is elegant and correctly derived. I agree. The derivations in App. A.1-A.2 follow directly, and the gradient analysis in Section 4 gives a concrete mechanistic reading. The paper links the implicit-reward weighting to the degeneration of unweighted unlikelihood (Table 3). The theory answers the obvious expressivity question: every equivalence class has a unique representative of the DPO form (Proposition 1). The practical payoff is a few lines of loss code (App. B) replacing a multi-model PPO pipeline. That is a substantial contribution in its own right. The controlled sentiment experiment is well designed and is the cleanest evidence for the claim that DPO optimizes the same objective better. Ground-truth reward is available, and DPO's frontier lies above PPO and even PPO-GT. R1 and R2 rightly…
