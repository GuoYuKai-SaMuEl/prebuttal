Summary

The submission proposes Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). It reparameterizes the reward in the KL-regularized RLHF objective (Eq. 3) as r = beta log(pi/pi_ref) + beta log Z(x) (Eq. 5). Under Bradley-Terry the partition function cancels (Eq. 6), so preference learning becomes a binary cross-entropy over policy log-ratios (Eq. 7). This removes the explicit reward model and the RL loop. Section 5 and Appendix A.5-A.6 argue that the reparameterization spans every reward equivalence class (Lemmas 1-2, Theorem 1) and selects a unique representative (Proposition 1). The experiments cover:

  • a controlled IMDb sentiment task with a reward-KL frontier (Figure 2 left);
  • TL;DR summarization with GPT-J, where DPO reaches about 61% vs PPO's 57% GPT-4 win rate at temperature 0 (Figure 2 right);
  • Anthropic-HH single-turn dialogue with Pythia-2.8B (Figure 3);
  • CNN/DailyMail transfer (Table 1: 0.36 vs 0.26 at temp 0, 0.31 vs 0.23 at temp 0.25);
  • a human study validating GPT-4 as judge (Table 2).

Consensus strengths (all three reviewers; I agree)

  1. The core derivation is correct, simple and consequential. Eqs. 4-7 follow from the standard closed-form optimum of the KL-regularized objective (Appendix A.1). The cancellation of Z(x) under Bradley-Terry (Appendix A.2) and Plackett-Luce (A.3) is sound. The resulting loss is roughly ten lines of code (Appendix B) and removes on-policy sampling and a separate reward network from training. None of the reviewers found a technical error in Section 4 or Appendix A, and I found none either.
