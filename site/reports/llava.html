Summary

The submission presents LLaVA-1.5, a set of modifications to the LLaVA visual-instruction-tuning recipe:

  • a two-layer MLP connector;
  • CLIP-ViT-L-336px;
  • a 665K public instruction mixture with academic VQA/OCR/region data and explicit response-formatting prompts (Table 7);
  • ShareGPT text data;
  • a 13B Vicuna-v1.5 LLM.

It reports strong results across academic and instruction-following benchmarks (Tables 3–4) with 558K + 665K samples and about 26 hours on 8×A100 (Sec. 3.3). It also explores a grid-based high-resolution variant (LLaVA-1.5-HD, Sec. 3.4 / App. A.1), LLM choice (Sec. 4.4), data subsampling (Sec. 5.1), hallucination (Sec. 5.2) and compositionality (Sec. 5.3).

Strengths (agreed by all three reviewers)

  1. A simple, reproducible, public-data baseline with large gains. Against original LLaVA, MME rises 809.6 → 1531.3 and MM-Vet 25.5 → 36.1 (Table 4). The models are competitive with or better than far more heavily pretrained systems:
    • VQAv2 80.0 for LLaVA-1.5-13B vs 78.2 for Qwen-VL-Chat (Table 3);
    • VizWiz 53.6 vs 38.9 (Table 3);
    • MMBench 67.7 vs 60.6 (Table 4).
  2. An actionable diagnosis of the short- vs long-answer conflict. The response-formatting prompt (Sec. 3.2, Tables 1, 8, 10) is supported in Table 2: MME goes 1197.0 → 1323.8 when the prompt is added on top of VQAv2.
  3. Unusually complete disclosure of the data mixture (Table 7), evaluation prompts (Table 8) and hyperparameters (Table 9).
  4. Useful practitioner findings. The HD global-context ablation (Sec. 4.2) improves GQA 62.9 → 63.8, MME 1425.8 → 1497.5 and MM-Vet 31.9 → 35.1. Sec. 5.1 reports that 50% of the data retains more than 98% of performance.
