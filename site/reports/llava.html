Summary

The submission presents LLaVA-1.5, a set of modifications to LLaVA: a two-layer MLP connector, a 665K instruction mixture that adds academic VQA/OCR/region data with explicit response-format prompts (Table 7), CLIP-ViT-L-336px, and Vicuna-v1.5. It also proposes a grid-based high-resolution variant (LLaVA-1.5-HD, Sec. 3.4). The authors report strong results with 558K pretraining and 665K finetuning samples and about 26 hours on 8 A100s (Sec. 3.3). The 13B model reaches 80.0 VQAv2, 63.3 GQA, 53.6 VizWiz, 71.6 SciQA-IMG and 61.3 TextVQA (Table 3), and 1531.3 MME and 67.7 MMBench (Table 4). The paper also explores LLM choice (Sec. 4.4), data subsampling (Sec. 5.1), hallucination (Sec. 5.2) and compositional behaviour (Sec. 5.3).

Strengths (agreed by all three reviewers)

  • Strong, efficient, reproducible baseline. Only public data is used. The full mixture (Table 7), evaluation prompts (Table 8), hyperparameters (Table 9) and greedy decoding are documented. Competing systems use 129M (InstructBLIP) to 1.4B (Qwen-VL) pretraining pairs.
  • Useful incremental ablation (Table 2). Adding VQAv2 raises MME from 809.6 to 1197.0. The format prompt raises it to 1323.8, which beats InstructBLIP's 1212.8 by about 111 points. The MLP connector raises it to 1355.2.
  • Clear diagnosis of the short- vs long-form answer tension. Sec. 3.2 and Table 1 explain the problem, and the format-prompt fix avoids ChatGPT rewriting of the data.
  • Measurable gains on held-out benchmarks. These include VizWiz, where unanswerable-question accuracy rises from 11.1% to 67.8% (Sec. 4.3), and MMBench-CN, where the model beats Qwen-VL-Chat 63.6 vs 56.7.
